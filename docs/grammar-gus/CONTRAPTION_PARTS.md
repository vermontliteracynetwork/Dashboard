# Gus's Workboard: Grammar Contraption Parts

A list of silly, physics-style, plug-and-play machine parts for Gus's Workboard. Each part does something funny on the board and has a real grammar job hidden inside it. Prepared by Claudia (game and instructional design review), 2026-10-07.

**Teacher's request:** "claudia, lets develop a list of features that can be physics/contraption/rube goldberg fun plug and play machine elements that are rooted in grammatical concepts"

**Teacher's direction for the whole Workboard:** "i want this to be a sandbox style for the kids to play around with grammar in the same STEAM style way they like to play other games, with hidden grammar concepts"

**Build tiers:**
- **NOW:** fits the current grammar engine.
- **SOON:** needs a small engine addition.
- **LATER:** needs grammar the engine does not teach yet.

## Rules every part follows

- **Wrong is private.** A wrong placement makes the part wobble, clunk or jam on the student's own screen only. Nobody else sees it.
- **Effort pays.** A fixed jam still earns the full run and the movie.
- **No gambling.** There are no spinning prize wheels, loot boxes or random rewards. The grammar causes every reaction.
- **iPad first.** Every part snaps on by tap or drag. Targets are 44px or larger, and nothing needs hover.
- **Set per student.** The teacher can turn parts on or off.
- **Silly is welcome.** Sounds, bounces, sparks and steam are all good.

## Already on the Workboard (shipped 2026-10-07)

Every fun part now has a grammar job:

| Part | What it does | Hidden grammar |
|---|---|---|
| Big Horn (new) | HONK! Blasts at the end | Exclamation point, or the "!" right after an interjection |
| Spring Mat | BOING! Bounces in a word | Adjective (describing word) |
| Fan | WHOOSH! Blows in a word | Adverb (how word) |
| Ramp | WHEEE! Rolls in a word | Preposition (where word) |
| Conveyor Belt | CLANK-CLANK, carries a word | Conjunction (joining word) |
| Bucket Drop | SPLOSH! Drops in a word | Article (a, an, the) |
| Pulley | HEAVE-HO! Hoists the next word | Capital letter |
| Bell | DING! | Period |
| Dominoes | CLACK-CLACK | Comma |
| Duplicator (new) | COPY! COPY! Copies the next noun | Plural noun, and the verb has to agree |

## 1. Capital letters and punctuation

**1. Proper Name Stamp** (SOON)
- **What it does:** A giant rubber stamp hangs over the pipe. When a name passes, it slams down and a gold capital letter pops up with a clang.
- **Grammar job:** Names of people, places and days start with a capital letter.
- **Trains:** Proper nouns.
- **Example:** "gus visited vermont" becomes "Gus visited Vermont."

**2. Mood Meter Valve** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A pressure gauge whose needle swings from "calm" to "BIG FEELING." It opens the Bell or the Big Horn.
- **Grammar job:** Matches the end punctuation to the sentence. Calm gets a period and a strong feeling gets an exclamation point.
- **Trains:** Choosing end punctuation on purpose.
- **Example:** "The roof is on fire." becomes "The roof is on fire!"

**3. Comma List Train** (SOON)
- **What it does:** A little train with one cart per item. Each coupler between carts is a comma, and the last cart hooks on with an "and" hook.
- **Grammar job:** Commas between items in a list.
- **Trains:** Commas in a series.
- **Example:** "I packed pizza chips and cake" becomes "I packed pizza, chips, and cake."

**4. Sentence Sprinter Flag** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A starting gun fires at the first word and a checkered flag drops at the last mark.
- **Grammar job:** A capital letter at the start and punctuation at the end.
- **Trains:** Sentence boundaries.
- **Example:** "the dog barked" gets a Pulley and a Bell, then the flag waves.

## 2. Nouns and articles

**5. Irregular Trapdoor** (SOON)
- **What it does:** The Duplicator's weird cousin. The marble drops through a trapdoor and comes out odd: a cartoon "mouses" gets crushed and "mice" comes out.
- **Grammar job:** Irregular plurals.
- **Trains:** Irregular plural nouns.
- **Example:** "two mouse" becomes "two mice."

**6. A/An Vowel Sniffer** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A vacuum nozzle on a spring sniffs the next word's first letter. A vowel sound makes it sneeze and swap the Bucket Drop's "a" for "an."
- **Grammar job:** a versus an.
- **Trains:** Articles before vowel sounds.
- **Example:** "a apple" becomes "an apple."

## 3. Pronouns

**7. Pronoun Teleporter** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A glowing yellow pad. When a noun repeats, the second one steps on it and zaps into a pronoun.
- **Grammar job:** Replaces a repeated noun with the right pronoun.
- **Trains:** Pronoun reference.
- **Example:** "Mia fed the cat. Mia smiled." becomes "Mia fed the cat. She smiled."

**8. Subject and Object Turnstile** (SOON)
- **What it does:** A two-way gate. The doer side lets I, he and she through. The receiver side lets me, him and her through. A wrong pronoun makes the gate spin and bonk.
- **Grammar job:** Pronoun case.
- **Trains:** Subject pronouns and object pronouns.
- **Example:** "Him threw the ball to I" becomes "He threw the ball to me."

**9. Possessive Tag Gun** (LATER)
- **What it does:** A sticker gun shoots a name tag onto an item.
- **Grammar job:** An apostrophe and "s" show who owns something.
- **Trains:** Possessives.
- **Example:** "the dogs bone" becomes "the dog's bone."

## 4. Verbs and time

**10. Agreement Gears** (NOW)
- **What it does:** The noun machine and the action machine each get a gear. Matching gears mesh with a smooth whir. A mismatch grinds and throws sparks.
- **Grammar job:** Subject and verb agreement.
- **Trains:** One and more than one.
- **Example:** "The dogs runs" becomes "The dogs run."

**11. Time Tunnel** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A three-lane tunnel with a past, present and future gate. The action machine changes shape as the marble passes through.
- **Grammar job:** The verb's time matches the Clock and any time word.
- **Trains:** Consistent verb tense.
- **Example:** "Last night, we walk" becomes "Last night, we walked."

**12. Irregular Past Press** (SOON)
- **What it does:** A heavy press. "goed" goes in and "went" gets stamped out.
- **Grammar job:** True irregular past forms.
- **Trains:** Irregular past verbs.
- **Example:** "He runned home" becomes "He ran home."

## 5. Adjectives and adverbs

**13. Describe Sorter Chute** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A chute with slots in order (opinion, size, color). The words roll in and clack into their slots.
- **Grammar job:** Describing words in the right order.
- **Trains:** Adjective order.
- **Example:** "a red big ugly truck" becomes "an ugly big red truck."

**14. How-Dial** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A big dial on the Fan. "slowly" makes the marble creep and "quickly" makes it zoom.
- **Grammar job:** How the action happens, with the adverb beside its verb.
- **Trains:** Adverbs of manner.
- **Example:** "The turtle moved slowly."

**15. Opener Slingshot** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A slingshot flings a how word to the front of the line, and a tiny comma domino tips behind it.
- **Grammar job:** An adverb opener with its comma.
- **Trains:** Sentence openers.
- **Example:** "Suddenly, the lights went out."

**16. Size-Up Inflator** (LATER)
- **What it does:** A bike pump. Each pump grows the describing word's balloon: tall, taller, tallest.
- **Grammar job:** Comparing forms.
- **Trains:** Comparatives and superlatives.
- **Example:** "Gus is the tall robot" becomes "Gus is the tallest robot."

## 6. Prepositions

**17. Where-To Switchyard** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A rail switch with three paths. The marble goes over a bridge, under a tunnel or through a ring, depending on the preposition.
- **Grammar job:** The preposition shows where.
- **Trains:** Position words.
- **Example:** "The cat jumped over the fence."

**18. Dead-End Detector** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A Ramp with no landing gets a "ROAD CLOSED" sign. Add a noun and the landing pad pops into place.
- **Grammar job:** Every preposition needs a noun after it.
- **Trains:** Complete prepositional phrases.
- **Example:** "The cat sat on." becomes "The cat sat on the mat."

## 7. Conjunctions

**19. Merge Funnel** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A big purple funnel. Two noun marbles pour in and one fat marble comes out.
- **Grammar job:** Two nouns joined by "and" count as more than one.
- **Trains:** Compound subjects.
- **Example:** "Tom and Mia run."

**20. Comma Hinge Drawbridge** (NOW)
- **What it does:** A drawbridge joins two sentence islands. It only lowers when a comma domino sits at the hinge, right before the joining word.
- **Grammar job:** The comma before and, but or so when two sentences join.
- **Trains:** Compound sentences.
- **Example:** "I was tired, but I kept going."

**21. Because Seesaw** (LATER)
- **What it does:** A seesaw with a cause on one end and an effect on the other. A "because" weight tips it.
- **Grammar job:** Linking a reason to a result.
- **Trains:** Subordinating conjunctions.
- **Example:** "We stayed in because it rained."

## 8. Interjections and sentence types

**22. Confetti Trapdoor** (NOW)
- **What it does:** An orange popper at the start of the pipe. "Wow!" pops up and confetti falls on the first machine.
- **Grammar job:** An interjection goes first, followed by punctuation.
- **Trains:** Interjections.
- **Example:** "Wow! The marble flew."

**23. Question Crane** (LATER)
- **What it does:** A hook picks up the action machine and swings it in front of the noun.
- **Grammar job:** Flips the word order and adds a question mark.
- **Trains:** Asking questions.
- **Example:** "You are ready." becomes "Are you ready?"

**24. Speech Bubble Blower** (LATER)
- **What it does:** A bubble pipe. The words inside get quotation marks, and a tag like "she said" follows.
- **Grammar job:** Quotation marks around spoken words.
- **Trains:** Dialogue punctuation.
- **Example:** "I'm done," she said.

## Chain reactions (combos)

- **Plural Pileup.** The Duplicator copies the noun, then the Agreement Gears grind until the verb changes to match: "one dog barks" becomes "two dogs bark."
- **Time Warp.** The Clock set to the future, the Time Tunnel and a time word like "Next" all have to agree. When they do, the movie plays.
- **Dramatic Entrance.** The Confetti Trapdoor, the Mood Meter Valve and the Big Horn: "Wow" opens the line, the gauge reads high, and the Horn blasts the exclamation point.
- **Big Merge.** The Merge Funnel, the Agreement Gears and the Comma Hinge Drawbridge: two nouns pour in, the verb gear shifts to plural, and the bridge lowers to join a second sentence.
- **Road Closed Repair.** The Dead-End Detector and the Where-To Switchyard: add the missing noun, the landing appears, and the marble goes over, under or through on the TV.

## Suggested build order

1. **First NOW batch:** Agreement Gears, Time Tunnel, Merge Funnel and the A/An Vowel Sniffer. These pay off the most and each shows grammar through motion.
2. **Second NOW batch:** the rest of the NOW parts.
3. **SOON batch:** Proper Name Stamp, Comma List Train, the Subject and Object Turnstile, and the two irregular parts.
4. **LATER:** after the engine teaches questions, possessives and quotation marks.
