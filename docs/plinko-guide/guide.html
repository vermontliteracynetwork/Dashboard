For students designing a Plinko theme

Plinko Game Guide

How a game goes, how the board works, what every power-up does, and how to plan your own power-ups so they fit the game.

The game at a glance

PartHow it works
Players1 to 5 on one iPad: you, classmates in the room, and Neighbors. Everyone takes turns.
Rounds3 to 10 (5 is the usual). One round means every player has had one turn.
Questions1 to 5 questions before each drop. A wrong answer brings a new question until one is right. Then you drop.
ScoreXP. The bucket your ball lands in is the XP you get. Most XP at the end wins.
MoneyEvery right answer still earns your usual class money.

One turn, step by step

  1. Whose turn. A big card shows the player and the round.
  2. Pick power-ups. Only if you won stars on your last drop. One star means you pick one power-up. You can save up to 3.
  3. Answer. Get the question right to earn your drop.
  4. Aim. Tap a numbered drop zone across the top of the board.
  5. Drop. The ball falls through 7 rows of pegs. On each row it bounces half a space left or right, by chance.
  6. Land. The bucket gives you XP. Stars on that bucket are power-ups for your next turn.
  7. Extra drops. If a power-up gave you one, you aim and drop again right away, with no new question.
  8. Next player. When everyone has gone, a new round starts and the board grows.

  • Bucket points are mixed into a new order at the start of every round, so the best bucket moves.
  • Stars: the top bucket never has stars. Smaller buckets are more likely to have stars (1 to 3). There is always at least one bucket with stars.
  • That makes aiming a choice: big points now, or power-ups for your next turn.

How points are counted

XP for a drop = bucket points × every multiplier, then + every bonus

Example: the ball lands in 30 with Double on and a +20 bonus: 30 × 2 = 60, then 60 + 20 = 80 XP.

The four main power-ups

Every power-up in every theme follows the same four rules: what it does, when it works, how the ball moves, and how it works with other power-ups. A theme can rename a main power-up and give it a new picture, but it always keeps its action.

All power-ups: picked at the start of your turn with a star from your last drop, on for this whole turn (extra drops included), then gone.

Double Multiply, times 2

What it does
The bucket counts twice. A 30 bucket gives 60.
When it works
Every drop this turn, extra drops too.
How the ball moves
No change.
With others
Multipliers multiply each other (times 2 and times 3 make times 6). Bonuses are added after, so they are never doubled.

Big Ball Big ball

What it does
The ball is bigger and heavier, so where you drop it matters more.
When it works
Every drop this turn.
How the ball moves
On each row there is a 1 in 2 chance it falls straight down instead of bouncing.
With others
On the rows where it does bounce, a Magnet can still pull it.

Magnet Pull to the top bucket

What it does
Pulls the ball toward the bucket worth the most points.
When it works
Every drop this turn.
How the ball moves
On each row there is a 3 in 4 chance it bounces toward the top bucket. It can still miss.
With others
With Big Ball, some rows fall straight and the magnet pulls on the others. Works with multipliers and bonuses.

Second Drop Extra drops, 1

What it does
After your ball lands, you aim and drop again. Each drop scores on its own.
When it works
Right after your first drop, with no new question.
How the ball moves
No change. You pick a new drop zone.
With others
Every other power-up stays on for the extra drop. Stars from any drop count for your next turn.

When power-ups mix

When several power-ups are on, the game always uses them in this order:

  1. The fall. On each row, Heavy, Big and Growing balls check first (fall straight?). If the ball bounces, it moves half a space (a full space if Extra bouncy), and Magnet decides the direction.
  2. The landing. Pop picks the best of the three closest buckets. Then Never the lowest bucket moves the ball over, if it is on.
  3. Multipliers. All of them multiply the bucket.
  4. Bonuses. All of them are added last.
  5. Two balls. Each ball does steps 1 to 4 on its own, and both scores add up.
  6. Extra drops. Start again from step 1, with the same power-ups on.

Neighbors

Neighbors answer by themselves, pick their power-ups at random, and drop in a random zone. They follow every other rule the same way you do.

Peg colors and music

A theme can have up to 20 peg types, each with its own color and sound (a bark, a boing). Each round one more type joins the board, picked at random. Sounds and music are just for fun: they never change the points.

Plan your theme

What every theme needs

  • A name and a square cover
  • At least 5 balls (up to 10)

What it can also have

  • Background, title, board and side panel pictures
  • Colors for buttons, text, pegs and buckets
  • New names and pictures for the 4 main power-ups
  • New power-ups of its own
  • Peg colors with sounds, and one background song

Good power-up rules

  • Say what it does in one short sentence.
  • It lasts one turn, then it is gone.
  • It helps, but nobody wins every time.
  • It fits the theme (a dog theme: Super Smell).
  • Nothing ever gets hurt. Things can bounce, wobble, poof or float.
  • Think about what happens when it is on with Double or Magnet.
